## Slide 2 · The reality today (~2 min)

In many banks and insurance companies — in emerging markets and elsewhere — critical work still runs on **manual steps**: email chains, spreadsheets, paper handoffs, duplicate data entry.

That is not a failure of intent. Teams are busy. Systems grew over years. Change is hard.

But the cost is real:

- **Slow turnaround** — clients wait; staff chase approvals  
- **Inconsistent decisions** — the same case handled differently depending on who is on shift  
- **Errors that scale with volume** — one typo in a payment or claim hurts more as you grow  
- **Knowledge trapped in people** — when someone leaves, the process leaves with them  

Digital channels and customer expectations are rising everywhere. Manual back-office processes become the bottleneck.

**Transition:** *“The good news is you do not need a five-year transformation to start. You need one workflow done properly.”*

## Slide 3 · What automation actually brings (~2 min)

When I say **automation** here, I mean **structured workflow automation** — not “replace everyone with AI.”

Concrete meaning:

- A **rule** runs first: amount over limit, missing document, fraud flag, etc.  
- Work **routes** to the right person or system automatically  
- **Notifications** replace chasing by phone and email  
- **Status** is visible — no “where is my claim?” black holes  

What that delivers:

| Benefit | What it means in practice |
|---------|---------------------------|
| **Speed** | Hours or days become minutes for routine steps |
| **Consistency** | Same rule, same path, every time |
| **Scale** | Handle more volume without linear headcount growth |
| **Focus** | Staff spend time on judgment, not copy-paste |

Automation does not remove humans from sensitive decisions. It removes **friction** around them.

## Slide 4 · Banking — where to start (~2 min)

Examples of high-value automation in **banking** (start with **one**):

- **Outbound payments** above a threshold → route for approval before execution  
- **KYC / AML** document checks → auto-route incomplete files, flag exceptions  
- **Limit or account status changes** → enforce dual control before the core system updates  
- **Reconciliation alerts** → surface mismatches to the right team with context  

These are not exotic. They are the flows auditors and regulators ask about first.

**Phrase:** *“Pick the workflow that keeps your ops or compliance team awake at night. That is your pilot.”*

## Slide 8 · When automation goes wrong (~2 min)

**Act 2 begins.**

Without governance, automation creates **new** failures:

- A **wrong payment** executes instantly — no natural pause to catch it  
- A **claim** is settled without the checks your policy requires  
- A **limit change** applies before anyone with authority has seen it  
- When someone asks *“Who authorized this on Tuesday at 2 p.m.?”* — the answer is scattered logs, tickets, and chat messages  

**Speed without proof is liability.**

This is not theoretical. It is why many automation projects stall: risk and compliance block them because **nobody wants to sign** without evidence.

## Slide 9 · The accountability gap (~2 min)

Three hidden costs when you automate without a control layer:

1. **Audit drag** — weeks reconstructing what happened from incomplete records  
2. **Committee paralysis** — risk boards say “no” to the next project because the last one had no trail  
3. **Reputational and regulatory exposure** — especially when client data and money move automatically  

Application logs tell you **something happened**. They rarely answer:

- **Which rule** applied?  
- **Which person** approved?  
- **Can you verify** the record was not altered later?  

That gap is what **Aegis** is built to close.

## Slide 10 · Regulatory and trust context (~2 min)

Regulated finance operates under frameworks that expect **accountability** — whether you automate or not.

Examples your audience may recognize:

- **POPIA** (South Africa) — lawful processing, security safeguards, accountability for personal information  
- **AML / KYC** obligations — know who approved exceptions  
- **Internal audit & SOC-style controls** — evidence that controls operated as designed  
- **EU AI Act, SOC 2 mappings** — increasingly relevant for groups with international operations  

You do not need to name every law in the room. The point is universal:

> **Regulators and boards do not ask “Did the robot run?” They ask “Who was responsible, and can you prove it?”**

Automation must produce **evidence**, not just efficiency.

## Slide 13 · What Aegis is (~2 min)

**Aegis** sits **before** a sensitive action executes.

Flow:

1. **Rule** evaluates — allow, deny, or require human approval  
2. **Human** approves or refuses if the rule requires it  
3. **Action** proceeds only after the gate clears  
4. **Every step** is recorded in a signed, append-only register  

Aegis is **not**:

- Core banking  
- A SIEM or log aggregator  
- A replacement for your compliance team  

Aegis **is** the **accountability layer** — rules, named approvers, cryptographic integrity, exports for audit.

**Keys (be precise if asked):**

- **Production / SDK:** BYOK — your signing keys stay in your KMS  
- **Orchestrators (n8n, etc.):** Workflow Bridge — no private key inside the orchestration tool  

*“The demo uses the orchestrator bridge. On your production payment API, the same rules apply; signing can stay with you.”*

## Slide 16 · Human approval (~1 min)

Governance means separating **“proposed by the system”** from **“authorized by a person.”**

- Alerts via email, Slack, PagerDuty, or SMS — your setup  
- Named approver, timestamp, linked to one trace  
- Refusal or timeout → action does not execute  
- Full history: approved, refused, expired — with the rule that applied  

This is what risk committees and internal audit actually need.
